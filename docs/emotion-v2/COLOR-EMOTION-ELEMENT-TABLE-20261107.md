# Nebula: colour–emotion–emission-line research and revised draft

Research date: 7 November 2026 (Europe/Zagreb). Research/design proposal only: no code, PR, voice pipeline, training data, deployment, or merge changed. Only this document was written.

## 1. Research summary

Colour–emotion associations are **many-to-many tendencies, not universal laws or proof that colours induce emotions**. [Jonauskaite et al. (2020), Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/5G3HXWNGZTT6ZXFIHTDP/full) studied 4,598 participants in 30 nations and 22 languages, finding high nation-to-global pattern similarity (mean r = .88), alongside linguistic/geographic differences; prominent associations included red–anger/love, yellow–joy, orange–amusement/joy, black–fear/sadness, and grey–sadness/disappointment. These are whole-pattern and colour-to-emotion results, not a universal ranking of every emotion’s favourite colour. [Adams & Osgood (1973)](https://doi.org/10.1177/002202217300400201) provided early cross-cultural affective-meaning evidence, while [Hupka et al. (1997)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022022197282002) directly found red/black–anger and black–fear across their studied nations, with culture-specific envy/jealousy colours. [Valdez & Mehrabian (1994)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/) show that brightness and saturation matter substantially, and [Ou et al. (2004)](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/a-study-of-colour-emotion-and-colour-preference-part-i-colour-emo/) found broadly shared activity/weight/heat dimensions but British–Chinese differences in tense–relaxed and preference ratings. Calm is reasonably associated with blue/blue-green, whereas tenderness→pink is an inference from love/gentleness, not a proven violet code. Amusement→orange and disappointment→grey have more direct multinational support than folk lists suggest; surprise and seductive have no established universal unique hue. Jonauskaite’s study explicitly reports Chinese white–sadness and Greek purple–sadness, in addition to black mourning, and discusses red as Chinese good fortune/wedding symbolism: neither “white always positive” nor “red always angry” is defensible.

## 2. Revised table — spectral truth separated from semantic rendering

**Important constraint:** no single emission line can be white, grey, black, pink, or brown. White/grey/pink require mixtures or desaturation; black is very low/no visible light. Therefore a perfect one-line-per-emotion spectral match is impossible for this evidence-based palette. The table assigns a distinct real line to every emotion, but explicitly marks arbitrary carrier lines and the display transformations needed. Those transformations are a semantic/illustrative shader, **not a claim about the intrinsic emission spectrum**. Forcing a match without that distinction would fabricate physics. Names below describe the spectral region; hex values are computed display approximations, not exact monochromatic reproductions. “Strong/moderate/weak” are qualitative synthesis labels, not invented study statistics.

All wavelengths are rounded nominal visible air-line values, used numerically as listed. Square brackets indicate forbidden transitions. Multiple emotions may share an element/ion, but all 13 line wavelengths are distinct. There is no natural one-element–one-emotion relationship: the semantic assignment is human-designed.

### Why there are carrier rows and laboratory fallbacks

- Neutral, fearful, sad and disappointed cannot satisfy their dominant achromatic targets with a single line. Their carrier IDs should not be mistaken for evidence-based hues. Tender pink also requires a mixture. The table is honest about these unavoidable exceptions rather than assigning “grey wavelengths.”
- The two Ne I lines are real atomic transitions, listed by NIST at 5944.8342 and 5852.4879 angstrom, respectively. They are used to reach orange/amber without relabelling red nebular lines as orange. They are **not claimed to be prominent ionised-nebula lines**. If a strict nebular-only mode is required, omit these two lab-inspired carriers and use a labelled false-colour warm continuum/composite; do not claim an orange single line has been found.
- A physically simulated nebula does not let [O III] 495.9 and 500.7 intensities vary independently arbitrarily: atomic branching, ionisation, excitation, density and abundance constrain emission. Emotion-layer isolation is an artistic abstraction, not an astrophysical evolution model. Dust extinction/scattering depends on grain sizes, composition, geometry and wavelength; a wavelength table alone does not implement those physics.

## 3. CIE 1931 → sRGB calculation

The hex values were actually computed with Python 3 + NumPy (no colour package installed), using the tabulated **CIE 1931 2-degree CMFs**, at 1 nm spacing from the [public munsellinterpol/CVRL-derived data mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/munsellinterpol/master/inst/extdata/ciexyz31_1.csv). Dataset provenance is documented in that CSV header (Wyszecki & Stiles / CVRL). The [official CIE dataset description](https://www.cie.co.at/datatable/cie-1931-colour-matching-functions-2-degree-observer) confirms the observer and 1 nm specification; direct retrieval at the guessed official CSV URL returned 404, so the mirror, **not an unverified official download**, was used. Downloads were processed in memory only.

For each wavelength lambda: linearly interpolate xbar, ybar, zbar, treating the line as a delta-function spectrum; obtain XYZ proportional to those CMFs. Multiply by the standard D65 XYZ→linear-sRGB matrix below; clamp negative components to zero (explicit approximate gamut clipping); normalise by the maximum remaining linear RGB component; apply the sRGB transfer function, then round to 8-bit RGB:

```text
M = [[ 3.2406, -1.5372, -0.4986],
     [-0.9689,  1.8758,  0.0415],
     [ 0.0557, -0.2040,  1.0570]]
rgb = max(M @ XYZ, 0)             # componentwise
rgb = rgb / max(rgb)             # equal peak-channel display normalisation
srgb(c) = 12.92*c                 if c <= 0.0031308
          1.055*c**(1/2.4)-0.055 otherwise
hex_channel = round(255*srgb(c))
```

These are **normalised, gamut-clipped spectral swatches**, not equal-radiance brightness predictions, not equal-luminance swatches, and not CIE colour names. Most monochromatic colours lie outside sRGB: pure spectral light cannot be reproduced exactly by an ordinary display. Normalising discards wavelength-dependent visibility, so violet/near-UV lines would be grossly over-brightened if used directly for physical luminosity. In particular, several red wavelengths collapse to `#FF0000` under this mapping; distinct line IDs do **not** guarantee distinct visible colours. Achromatic/pink modifications in the table are not included in the spectral hex. Never substitute made-up hex values while claiming they came directly from monochromatic CIE calculations.

## 4. Changes from the previous draft

- **Neutral:** H-alpha red → H-delta carrier plus explicitly achromatic treatment; no evidence that neutral means red, and no physically neutral single line exists.
- **Calm:** H-beta retained; cyan/blue-green broadly fits calm.
- **Tender:** H-gamma violet → He I 667.8 red carrier plus white mixture for pink. Affection-based inference replaces unsupported universal violet.
- **Happy:** He I 587.6 retained. Its traditional “yellow” name is not permission to label the computed orange-amber hex as pure yellow.
- **Excited:** [O III] 500.7 teal-green → Ne I 594.5 orange, explicitly a lab fallback and an arousal-family inference.
- **Amused:** near-UV [O II] 372.7 → Ne I 585.2 amber-orange. Orange–amusement has direct multinational support; this is not just a weak folk claim.
- **Surprised:** Ne I 640.2 red → [O III] 495.9 cyan as a labelled arbitrary design choice. No robust evidence determines a unique surprise colour.
- **Fearful:** He II 468.6 retained only as a dim carrier; darkness, not “hot blue,” carries the evidence-based meaning. Colour is not a gas-temperature readout.
- **Sad:** Ar I 696.5 deep red → H-gamma violet-blue carrier with dark/grey treatment; the most supported global target is black/grey, with blue and mourning-colour variants secondary.
- **Disappointed:** [S II] 673.1 red → [Cl III] 551.7 carrier desaturated to grey, matching the semantic finding but explicitly not the raw line colour.
- **Angry:** [S III] 631.2 retained as red. Calling 631.2 “deeper red” than 673.1 is backwards: both are red and 673.1 is longer-wavelength.
- **Disgusted:** N II 399.5 violet-blue → [Cl III] 553.8 yellow-green; acknowledge brown/green-yellow and cultural variability rather than a universal green law.
- **Seductive:** [O I] 630.0 red-orange → H-alpha 656.3 red for love/passion symbolism, explicitly only a weak inference for seduction. Samantha-only is a product rule, not a psychological result.

## 5. Source register and evidence boundaries

1. **Primary multinational study:** [Jonauskaite et al. 2020, Universal Patterns in Color-Emotion Associations Are Further Shaped by Linguistic and Geographic Proximity](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/5G3HXWNGZTT6ZXFIHTDP/full), Psychological Science, DOI 10.1177/0956797620948810. Full text read. Its “prominent” pair list uses estimated probabilities >= .4, but those are colour-to-emotion endorsements, not exclusive emotion-to-colour winners. No per-row invented percentage is supplied here. Amusement and disappointment are directly included; neutral, calm, tender, excited, surprised and seductive should not be equated mechanically with that list’s other emotion terms.
2. **Early primary study:** [Adams & Osgood 1973, A Cross-Cultural Study of the Affective Meanings of Color](https://doi.org/10.1177/002202217300400201). Used for historical context only; not used to invent discrete-emotion rankings. Secondary contextual summary: [Fugate & Franco 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6399154/), What Color Is Your Anger? (search-index text was accessible; direct fetch returned 403).
3. **Primary dimensional experiment:** [Valdez & Mehrabian 1994, Effects of color on emotions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/), DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.123.4.394. Abstract read via indexed PubMed content: brightness/saturation effects concern pleasure–arousal–dominance, not all 13 named emotions.
4. **Primary cross-cultural anger/fear study:** [Hupka et al. 1997, The Colors of Anger, Envy, Fear, and Jealousy](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022022197282002). Indexed primary abstract read. Supports anger red/black and fear black, and warns against universal envy-colour claims; envy is not disgust.
5. **Primary dimensional colour study:** [Ou, Luo, Woodcock & Wright 2004, Part I: Colour emotions for single colours](https://doi.org/10.1002/col.20010); [institutional abstract](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/a-study-of-colour-emotion-and-colour-preference-part-i-colour-emo/). Primary abstract read through web results: 31 observers (14 British, 17 Chinese); dimensional scales are not proof of a universal categorical emotion palette.
6. **Cross-national calm context:** [Madden, Hewett & Roth 2000, Managing Images in Different Cultures](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jimk.8.4.90.19795). Calming blue/green/white pattern taken from the Fugate & Franco literature-summary table, not claimed as a newly inspected raw-data analysis.
7. **Primary cultural comparison:** [Jonauskaite et al. 2019, A machine learning approach to quantify the specificity of colour–emotion associations and their cultural differences](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6774957/). Indexed primary text read: brown–disgust varied markedly between Germany and China; purple–sadness in Greece; white more negative in China.
8. **Seduction caution:** [Do red objects enhance sexual attractiveness? No evidence from two large replications](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.displa.2018.10.008); [Revisiting the Red Effect on Attractiveness and Sexual Receptivity](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10630698/). These concern attraction/receptivity, not a validated cross-cultural “seductive emotion” hue. Indexed titles/results used as caution only, not as evidence that red cannot symbolise passion.
9. **Broader synthesis:** [Do we feel colours? A systematic review of 128 years of psychological research linking colours and emotions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12325498/). Indexed conclusion supports many-to-many correspondences and effects of lightness/saturation; conceptual associations do not establish experienced emotion causation.
10. **Atomic/nebular line checks:** [NIST Strong Lines of Neon](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/neontable2_a.htm), full table read; [Emission Lines Identified in Planetary Nebulae](https://laserstars.org/data/nebula/identification.html), full table read, citing Acker et al. 1989 / Meinel et al. 1975. The latter is a historical compiled identification list, not a modern precision wavelength standard; use rounded nominal values for this visual design, not precision spectroscopy.
