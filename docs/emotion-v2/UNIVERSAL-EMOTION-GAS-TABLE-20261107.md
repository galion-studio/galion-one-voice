# Universal emotion–gas design vocabulary and Demo 13 rows v2

7 November 2026 (Europe/Zagreb). Research proposal only. No source-code edits, voice generation, merge or deployment.

## Scope: “universal” is a coverage goal, not an established scientific fact

There is no agreed exhaustive universal inventory of every human emotion, and no validated universal emotion→gas relationship. Here “Universal table” means an extensible **cross-taxonomy design vocabulary**, covering all requested categories, GEW 3, GoEmotions and Cowen–Keltner categories with documented aliases. Categories and colours are not evidence that the same discrete emotions or meanings occur identically in every culture. Neutral is a baseline, and “seductive” is a product label, not a universal basic emotion. A fixed table is a software convention, not a biological or astronomical discovery.

### Taxonomy sources

- **C17:** [Cowen & Keltner 2017, Self-report captures 27 distinct categories of emotion bridged by continuous gradients](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5617253/), DOI [10.1073/pnas.1702247114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1702247114). Primary indexed abstract and category material consulted; direct PMC fetch was blocked. Distinguish the 34 candidate labels rated in their task from the 27 dimensions retained. We include the retained categories plus requested candidates; we do not claim 27 independent universal basic emotions. [Berkeley’s institutional account](https://news.berkeley.edu/2017/09/06/27-emotions/) provides contextual confirmation.
- **G20:** [Demszky et al. 2020, GoEmotions](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.372/), primary abstract, plus [documented complete label list](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/goemotions). English Reddit annotation categories are a computational taxonomy, not a globally representative colour-emotion study.
- **GEW:** [Scherer et al. 2013, The GRID meets the wheel](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:97384), [official research-materials hub](https://www.unige.ch/cisa/research/materials-and-online-research/research-material). The 20 GEW categories used in J20 are covered: interest, amusement, pride, joy, pleasure, contentment, love, admiration, relief, compassion, sadness, guilt, regret, shame, disappointment, fear, disgust, contempt, hate, anger.
- Aliases used for coverage, not assertions of complete psychological equivalence: Happy/joy; Calm/calmness; Amused/amusement; Excited/excitement; Sad/sadness; Disappointed/disappointment; Angry/anger; Fearful/fear; Surprised/surprise; Disgusted/disgust; Tender/tenderness; Interest/curiosity. Nervousness and anxiety, remorse and guilt/regret, and love/adoration/romance remain separate where taxonomies distinguish them.

### Colour evidence and its limits

**J20:** [Jonauskaite et al. 2020, Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/eprint/5G3HXWNGZTT6ZXFIHTDP/full), full text read: 4,598 participants, 30 nations, 22 languages, whole-pattern nation-to-global mean r=.88. Prominent pairings include red–anger/love, yellow–joy, orange–amusement/joy, pink–love/pleasure/joy, grey–sadness/disappointment, black–fear/sadness/hate and white–relief. This is not an exclusive ranking of every emotion’s favourite colour. White–sadness in China, purple–sadness in Greece and Chinese red good-fortune symbolism are explicitly discussed. Egyptian yellow–joy differed from the global pattern.

**P20:** [Jonauskaite et al. 2020, i-Perception, Feeling Blue or Seeing Red?](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2041669520902484), full text read: French-speaking student term/patch comparison, **not** the 30-nation study. Direct results additionally support grey/black–regret, black–contempt and pink–pleasure; guilt has a reported negative black association. Pink–pride is explicitly an example of a rare pairing, so gold/purple/pink pride is not promoted to a universal result. Shame, pride, guilt, contentment, compassion and admiration being measured in GEW does not establish a strong unique colour for each.

**H97:** [Hupka et al. 1997](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022022197282002), indexed primary abstract: anger red/black, fear black, jealousy red, envy markedly culture-dependent. **J19:** [Jonauskaite et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6774957/) documents brown–disgust differences, notably Germany versus China. **F19:** [Fugate & Franco 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00206/full), indexed study/literature-summary evidence, supports local English-language tendencies, not a universal embarrassment/boredom mapping.

Classic context: [Adams & Osgood 1973](https://doi.org/10.1177/002202217300400201); **V94** [Valdez & Mehrabian 1994](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/) on brightness/saturation and pleasure/arousal/dominance; **O04** [Ou et al. 2004](https://pureportal.coventry.ac.uk/en/publications/a-study-of-colour-emotion-and-colour-preference-part-i-colour-emo/) on activity/weight/heat, with British–Chinese tense–relaxed differences; [Madden et al. 2000](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jimk.8.4.90.19795) calming blue/green/white context (via F19 review table). These cannot manufacture precise categorical colours for untested emotions. [Large red-attraction replications](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.displa.2018.10.008) caution against equating red symbolism with a reliable seduction effect.

**Evidence labels:** strong = directly prominent multinational evidence, sometimes convergent classic studies; moderate = direct narrower-sample evidence or broad dimensional/convergent context; weak = local, culturally variable or explicitly family-based inference; none = no adequate category-specific association located in reviewed sources, so the proposed colour is a **design choice**, not proof no such research exists. Labels are qualitative, not study percentages. For each row the evidence applies to the human association, never to a natural emotion–element link.

## Physics and spectral-hex method

All tabulated wavelengths are rounded nominal air-line values in nm. I=neutral atom, II=singly ionised, III=doubly ionised, IV=triply ionised. Brackets indicate forbidden transitions; no brackets on selected H/He/Ne lines indicate permitted transitions. H I/He I/He II line names refer to the emitting atom/ion (He II is He+), not the recombining parent ion.

- H-alpha is red; [O I] 630.0 is conventionally red-orange and appears red after this gamut clipping. Ne I visible lines are predominantly yellow-orange through red; selected 594.5 is orange, not blue.
- Neutral Ar I has strong red/near-IR lines such as 696.5, 750.4 and 763.5; it is **not** our blue argon. Selected blue [Ar IV] 474.0 is Ar3+ forbidden emission in high-excitation gas. Ar II lamp blue lines would be a different ion and environment.
- H II/PNe means ionised emission nebulae/planetary nebulae. Pure reflection nebulae mainly scatter a star’s continuum and inherited spectral features; they do not manufacture these local ionic emission lines. Absorption needs a background source and suitable level populations; a line’s appearance in emission elsewhere does not establish it as typical interstellar absorption. None of these rows is presented as a reflection-generated or universally absorbing line.
- Typical strengths below are qualitative relative to optical nebular lines, not universal ratios: abundance, excitation, density, temperature, extinction and aperture change them. Weak auroral/trace-ion lines must not be made intrinsically bright and called realistic without an explicit display gain. [O III] 495.9/500.7 share atomic constraints; emotion controls cannot vary all atomic populations independently in a physically faithful model.
- Grey/white/black/pink/brown are **non-spectral**: desaturate or add broadband components, dim, mix white, or render low-luminance orange in context. A carrier wavelength for one of these targets is an explicit arbitrary ID, not a grey/white/black/pink/brown gas line.

Physics sources: [Peimbert et al., Nebular Spectroscopy: A Guide on H II Regions and Planetary Nebulae](https://www.astrosen.unam.mx/~richer/docencia/astrofisica1/hii_pn_spectroscopy_peimbertetal17.pdf), indexed primary review text; [historical planetary-nebula identifications](https://laserstars.org/data/nebula/identification.html), full table; [actual PN spectrum and line intensities](https://danehkar.net/papers/2013mnras/node5.html), indexed table (not claimed representative of every nebula); [NIST neon line list](https://physics.nist.gov/PhysRefData/Handbook/Tables/neontable2_a.htm), full table read previously, 5944.8342 Å and 5852.4879 Å atomic lines.

Spectral hex was recomputed using the **same method as the first report**: Python 3/NumPy; linearly interpolate the 1 nm tabulated [CIE 1931 2-degree CMFs from the CVRL-derived mirror](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cran/munsellinterpol/master/inst/extdata/ciexyz31_1.csv); monochromatic XYZ = (xbar,ybar,zbar); multiply by D65 XYZ-to-linear-sRGB matrix [[3.2406,-1.5372,-0.4986],[-0.9689,1.8758,0.0415],[0.0557,-0.2040,1.0570]]; clamp negatives to zero, divide by maximum channel; apply sRGB transfer at 0.0031308 (12.92c below; 1.055c^(1/2.4)-0.055 above); round to 8-bit. [CIE dataset description](https://www.cie.co.at/datatable/cie-1931-colour-matching-functions-2-degree-observer). These are gamut-clipped equal-peak swatches, **not exact spectral reproduction, radiance or equal luminosity**. Spectral reds can legitimately collapse to #FF0000; one must not fabricate different computed hex values. Display targets below are explicitly hand-chosen semantic swatches, not additional CIE computations.
